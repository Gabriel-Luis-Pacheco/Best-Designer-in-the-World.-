# Research atlas — multidisciplinary design, copy, UX, marketing, sales and landing pages

**Research date:** 2026-11-11. **Mode:** public primary articles, publisher/author descriptions, accessible book excerpts, professional opinion, current guidelines and community discussion. The research below was gathered by public web access and is **not an assertion that full paid books were read or that any app/site was usability-tested**. Links are starting points; reread relevant sections at use time.

## Findings that change how we design

1. **Visual quality is concrete craft, not adjectives.** NN/g defines hierarchy through color/contrast, scale and grouping; The Futur foregrounds typography, alignment, proportion, balance and optical decisions; Refactoring UI proposes teachable design tactics. A concrete screenshot and rendered comparison usually constrain the solution better than “premium and clean”.
2. **Product-led proof bridges art and conversion.** A visually distinctive scene still needs to show what the actual service does. MarketingExperiments/MECLABS and Dunford both emphasize understandable value against alternatives, not generic grandiosity. Product truth is non-negotiable.
3. **The words should come from customers, not model intuition.** Copyhackers prioritizes interviews, on-site data, reviews and competitor audits; Dunford positioning begins with competitive alternatives; Strategyzer demands important jobs/pains/gains for a real audience. If there is no customer evidence, a headline is a hypothesis.
4. **"Shorter copy always converts" is false.** Unbounce's 2024 report correlates simpler copy and higher conversion in its dataset; correlation is not causation and some purchases require thorough explanations, terms and objections.
5. **Brand is not equal to immediate activation.** Ehrenberg-Bass emphasizes recognizable memory structures and availability; IPA distinguishes brand-building and short-term activation. A mascot, visual signature and campaign consistency matter, but small startups should not import mature-brand budget allocation ratios blindly.
6. **A great design must remain usable.** Norman's signifiers/feedback, Krug's meaningful choices, Tidwell's contextual patterns and NN/g heuristics reject a design that hides basic actions beneath cinematic animation.
7. **Visual fads are not creative strategy.** Webflow's 2026 design trends and curated gallery reports can identify professional taste, but they are editorial/selected examples. A custom, ownable visual system is more useful than mechanically forcing 3D, dark mode or oversized fonts.
8. **Test decisions with users and actual data.** Hopkins argued for market tests in 1923; NN/g advocates recurring small formative usability cycles; current performance/accessibility guidelines need measured checks. A model's self-awarded 10/10 grade is not evidence.

## Source map with status, finding, limit and application

### Design and aesthetics
- **Nielsen Norman Group — "5 Principles of Visual Design in UX" / "Visual Hierarchy in UX"** (2019/2021, publicly read): scale, hierarchy, balance, contrast and Gestalt; grouping, contrast and scale are cues for attention. **Apply:** review screen at full size and blurred thumbnail to see attention order; adjust type/media ratios. **Limit:** heuristics do not prove users prefer this exact art direction. Sources: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/principles-visual-design/ ; https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/
- **Chris Do, The Futur — typography fundamentals** (public article read): legibility, alignment, contrast, spacing, scale, proportion and balance as craft levers. **Apply:** audit line wrapping, type role and optical spacing on actual breakpoints. **Limit:** education/opinion, not causal CRO data. https://thefutur.com/content/learn-typography
- **Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger — Refactoring UI** (official product/intro read, **full book not accessed**): developer-friendly practical craft. **Apply:** concrete corrections instead of aesthetic adjectives. **Limit:** do not impose a single aesthetic/template. https://refactoringui.com/
- **Webflow — "8 web design trends to watch in 2026"** (2026, public article read): proprietary effects/styles, editorial restraint and distinctive visual systems respond to AI sameness. **Apply:** create brand-specific gesture/material visual signature; explore selectively. **Limit:** trend reporting and commercial opinion, not measured conversion. https://webflow.com/blog/web-design-trends-2026
- **Landdding — "The State of Landing Pages 2026"** (April 2026, reviewed overview): analysis of curated submission sample, reported common SaaS dark-mode/motion trends. **Apply:** contextual inspiration only. **Limit:** curated-site selection bias and original source claims need independent verification. https://landdding.com/state-of-landing-pages-2026
- **Community critiques of AI sameness — r/webdesign / r/UI_Design** (2025–2026, sampled user discussions): repeated gradients, uniform feature cards, templated SaaS structures and anonymous-feeling pages. Some users push back against calling all shadows/cards “AI slop.” **Apply:** avoid automatic defaults, not entire CSS techniques; use reference-led original art direction. **Limit:** anecdotes, no population statistics. https://www.reddit.com/r/webdesign/comments/1qqxq4i/website_design_feels_like_everyone_just_copies/ ; https://www.reddit.com/r/UI_Design/comments/1w70kqb/question_about_websites_looking_like_ai_slop/

### Product experience and UX
- **Don Norman — The Design of Everyday Things** (book concept and public reference/explanations read, **not full book**): affordances vs visible signifiers, feedback, mapping and constraints. **Apply:** actual affordance of voice/start button, microphone state, interaction results, error recovery. **Limit:** context matters. https://assets.interaction-design.org/literature/topics/don-norman
- **Steve Krug — Don't Make Me Think, Revisited** (publisher excerpt reviewed; **not full book**): unambiguous choice and information scent matter more than an absolute three-click limit. **Apply:** inspect CTA wording and navigation certainty. **Limit:** excerpt, not all chapters. https://www.peachpit.com/content/images/9780321965516/samplepages/0321965515.pdf (publisher PDF; do not claim read unless inspected with PDF tool). General author info: https://www.peachpit.com/authors/bio/c5794d61-cd5b-4c75-a976-4a3b1c475f4f
- **Jenifer Tidwell, Charles Brewer, Aynne Valencia — Designing Interfaces (3rd ed.)** (publisher contents, descriptions, accessible chapter excerpt, **not full book**): patterns resolve contextual tensions and require judgment, not prefabricated screens. **Apply:** choose patterns by task, adapt states and options. https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/designing-interfaces-3rd/9781492051954/ ; https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/designing-interfaces/0596008031/pr02s02.html
- **NN/g — 10 Usability Heuristics; Recognition and Recall; First Impressions; Five Users** (articles read): visible state/feedback, recognition over memory, early visceral impressions; small iterative qualitative tests find many issues, **not** population conversion estimates. **Apply:** comprehension check, real users, keyboard/task review. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/ ; https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall/ ; https://www.nngroup.com/articles/first-impressions-human-automaticity/ ; https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/
- **Baymard Institute — research-validated ecommerce UX** (public overview read): checkout, navigation, product and mobile patterns from large-scale tests. **Apply** to checkout/trust/commerce, not to every SaaS hero. **Limit:** ecommerce population, partial detailed access. https://baymard.com/product
- **W3C — WCAG 2.2** (public standard updates read): perceivable, operable, understandable, robust; specific target/focus/dragging guidance. **Apply:** functional a11y checks, no claim of conformance based on one automated scan. https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/

### Positioning and value
- **April Dunford — Obviously Awesome** (author's description and updated-edition announcement read, **not full book**): competitive alternatives, unique capabilities, value, target audience and market category should anchor messaging. **Apply:** “for whom, compared to what, what proven difference?”; don't lead with generic AI. **Limit:** originating examples often B2B; adapt to B2C without assuming fit. https://www.aprildunford.com/books ; https://www.aprildunford.com/
- **April Dunford — Sales Pitch** (author's description/positioning-vs-strategy post read, **not full book**): a customer's present purchase decision differs from an investor's future story. **Apply:** landing storytelling helps compare present alternatives, not vision slides. https://www.aprildunford.com/about ; https://www.aprildunford.com/post/positioning-vs-strategy-vs-vision
- **Alexander Osterwalder / Strategyzer — Value Proposition Canvas, 2026 value-propositions review** (official methods/articles read): important jobs, pains and gains in a segment; value must be perceptible to customer and validated. **Apply:** job-to-proof mapping, avoid broad “everyone” buyer. https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-value-proposition-canvas ; https://www.strategyzer.com/library/great-value-propositions
- **Bob Moesta / The Re-Wired Group — JTBD / demand-side sales** (public learn index/guides reviewed, **not full books**): buying vs using, why a customer “hires” a solution and switching tradeoffs. **Apply:** ask what triggers starting/quitting a speaking app; observe non-consumption. https://therewiredgroup.com/learn/

### Copy, persuasion and sales
- **Joanna Wiebe / Copyhackers — Conversion Copywriting and research** (original public article read): research/discovery → writing/wireframing/editing → validation; voice-of-customer and competitor audits. **Apply:** copy claims/proof based on actual users, not clever inventions. https://copyhackers.com/2022/06/copywriting-research/ ; https://copyhackers.com/3-part-conversion-copywriting-process-for-newbies/
- **Claude Hopkins — Scientific Advertising (1923)** (**accessible excerpts of chapters Headlines, Being Specific, Art in Advertising, Test Campaigns read; other chapters not read in this review**): select relevant readers, make verifiable specific claims, make visuals earn their space, test with actual buyers. **Apply:** observable proof and experiments, *not* fictional numbers or unsupported universal predictive certainty. 1923 assumptions about gender/creative restraint are not modern ethical defaults. https://www.holloway.com/g/scientific-advertising/sections/headlines ; https://www.holloway.com/g/scientific-advertising/sections/being-specific ; https://www.holloway.com/g/scientific-advertising/sections/art-in-advertising ; https://www.holloway.com/g/scientific-advertising/sections/test-campaigns ; public-domain original: https://www.loc.gov/item/23009362/
- **Chip Heath and Dan Heath — Made to Stick** (publisher description read, **not full book**): memorable communication uses concrete ideas, surprises, stories and other devices. **Apply:** one truthful product story instead of generic lists; test comprehension. https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/77687/made-to-stick-by-chip-heath-and-dan-heath/9781588365965/
- **Michael Masterson and John Forde — Great Leads** (third-party descriptive overview, **full book not read**; separately covered in existing Design OS \`great-leads-ethical-copy-framework.md\`): opening style and level of buyer awareness determine the hook. **Apply:** adapt chosen lead to actual promise, no rigid formula. https://www.theoldseller.com/great-leads
- **Robert Cialdini — seven persuasion principles** (official explanation read): reciprocity, scarcity, authority, consistency, liking, social proof, unity. **Apply:** voluntary authentic trial, honest customer evidence and clear choice; **never fake scarcity/reviews/urgency**. https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/
- **MarketingExperiments / MECLABS — conversion heuristic, clarity experiments** (original public material read): motivation, value, incentives/friction and anxiety are useful lenses, not numeric forecasts. **Apply:** identify and repair precise conversion blockers; read original case/context. https://marketingexperiments.com/value-proposition/optimizing-your-landing-pages ; https://beta.meclabs.com/patented-heuristic/
- **Harry Dry / Marketing Examples — practical landing guide** (public article read): communicate value, make mechanism visible, make it believable, easy action; acknowledges guide isn't universal. **Apply:** inspect every section's commercial job; don't force prescribed FAQ/logos. https://marketingexamples.com/content/guide

### Brand, acquisition, testing and technical fitness
- **Ehrenberg-Bass Institute — How Brands Grow; Building Distinctive Brand Assets** (official book descriptions/articles read, **not full books**): mental availability, physical/digital availability, distinct visual/memory cues and consistent identity. **Apply:** consistent approved mascot/visual language, discoverability and ease of access; do not equate pretty UI with brand penetration. https://marketingscience.info/learn-with-us/books ; https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/mental-availability-is-not-awareness-brand-salience-is-not-awareness
- **IPA / Les Binet & Peter Field — The Long and the Short of It** (official presentation summary and contextual caveat read, **not full paid report**): separate long-term brand outcomes and short-term activation. **Apply:** two measurement horizons; don't prescribe “60:40” to a cash-constrained startup. https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/documents/the-long-and-the-short-of-it-presentation ; https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/three-common-mistakes-in-awards-entries/
- **Unbounce — 2024 Conversion Benchmark Report / 2025 article** (vendor dataset methods/summary read): dataset 41K pages, 464M visitors, 57M conversions; cross-industry median around 6.6%, with observational associations between simpler copy and conversion. **Apply:** channel/intent-specific hypotheses, not universal target or causal guarantee. https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/ ; https://unbounce.com/average-conversion-rates-landing-pages/
- **web.dev / Google Search Central — Core Web Vitals** (official guides read): LCP <=2.5s, INP <=200ms, CLS <=0.1 good *field p75* thresholds. **Apply:** image/font/script and real-device performance work. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals ; https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds

## High-value disputes, not false consensus

| Tension | What the research supports | Design OS decision |
|---|---|---|
| Minimal copy vs tell the full story | Hopkins wants sufficient convincing argument; Unbounce observational data favors simple wording. | Distill repetition; preserve proof, terms, pricing and serious objections. |
| Awwwards artistry vs clarity/CRO | Visual recognition can matter; conversion needs legible value and action. | Show real product through branded art; measure each goal separately. |
| Customer research vs ship today | Real customer language is best, but teams sometimes have no interviews. | Build based on verified product facts; label unvalidated target message HYPOTHESIS; test as soon as users exist. |
| "AI slop" bans vs design craft | Community criticizes sameness rather than all cards/gradients. | Test whether design is interchangeable; ban defaults only when they undermine product identity. |
| Brand building vs short-term sales | Different mechanisms, different timescales. | Retain recognizable brand while designing direct activation; no universal allocation rule. |
| Fewer clicks vs clear choices | Krug favors unambiguous information scent, not rigid click count. | Verify users understand what each click will do. |
| Persuasion tricks vs authentic trust | Authority/scarcity/social proof can influence. | Use only truthful evidence; never invent or manipulate. |

## Field research and artifact contract

For substantial LPs with available runtime, minimum deliverable is not a research dossier. **Make an actionable evidence packet**:
- Product truth (verified route, action, media, price) and audience/job assumption.
- One defensible competitive frame: who is compared with whom and why.
- Observed visual sources: URL, screenshot, desktop/mobile, exact scene mechanism.
- Claim → proof → scene → CTA mapping with truth/rights annotations.
- Brand/creative signature and 2–3 source-grounded craft decisions.
- Real implemented full-page captures and one repaired weak scene.
- Functional CTA to first value, accessibility and performance checks.
- One measured or testable **qualified** outcome; unknown baselines labeled UNKNOWN.

For narrow PATCH, keep the scope narrow. **Do not replace implementation with reading.**

## Unresolved research gaps
- These sources do not establish that an agent with this skill designs better pages than agents without it. Run controlled builds and blind reviews.
- No independent user research was performed on any specific target product as part of this synthesis.
- None of the consulted authors provides a universal best hero, CTA color, animation engine or number of sections.
- Older direct-mail and Western brand research may not extrapolate to a Brazilian voice-learning SaaS without validation.
- The 2026 AI website sameness discussions are qualitative, self-selected online discourse.
