# Human comprehension and first-value gate

Use for a public landing, pricing, school/business lead flow, signup or onboarding when attracting the *right* visitors and making a real first use possible matter. Skip for pure art, internal technical patches or unrelated visual cleanup. This does not claim to have interviewed users.

## Make a page legible to the actual buyer

Define in one sentence: **who** is the visitor, **what situation brought them**, **what outcome they seek**, **what replaces this product**, and **what they must do next**. Distinguish facts learned from customers from positioning hypotheses. Avoid SaaS jargon, invented guarantees, fake logos and artificial urgency.

Inspect the first viewport and ask: can a new visitor tell, using the actual pixels, (1) what this is, (2) who it is for, (3) what they will receive or do, (4) why they should believe it, (5) what clicking the primary CTA really commits them to? An agent may make a *heuristic prediction*, but only representative participants can establish observed comprehension.

## Show rather than assert

Choose the most credible proof medium: real interactive sample, truthful screenshot, actual output, understandable workflow, licensed demonstration, real customer case with permission, or an explicitly labeled concept. Product demonstrations must match currently implemented behavior. A promised free trial is not evidence when the customer never reaches actual core value.

For every major scene, map `visitor question → evidence/new understanding → next action`. Merge or cut repetition that fails to change the visitor's beliefs. Do not remove legitimate privacy, price or eligibility information to shorten the page.

Follow the actual conversion path: marketing claim → CTA → destination → consent/auth/pricing (if applicable) → first meaningful value. Inspect normal, loading, error, mobile and keyboard states where relevant. A button click alone is not a validated acquisition funnel.

## Human test when permission, budget and participants are available

Show the page cold to 3–5 relevant people without revealing desired answers. Ask open questions: “O que é isto?”, “Para quem serve?”, “O que acontece ao clicar?”, “O que te deixa em dúvida?”; observe the first-use task without coaching. Capture verbatim answers and confusion themes, protect privacy, and separate different audience segments. This tiny qualitative sample identifies failure modes; it does not prove conversion lift or a statistically representative rate.

**If real participants aren't available:** record `HUMAN_COMPREHENSION_NOT_TESTED` and the agent's explicit hypotheses; never invent participants, quotes, percentages or a passing five-second test.

## Final decision record

Summarize what changed, what proof was observed, CTA→first-value path results, desktop/mobile screenshots, unresolved visitor questions, and whether the observations came from real participants. A design can be polished but confusing; a page can be understandable but visually forgettable. Track both independently.
