# Landing pages: conversion, product truth, story, and experimentation

Load for a commercial landing, waitlist, launch, pricing, acquisition or experiential-commerce funnel. Marketing evidence is distinct from artistic award criteria. A beautiful page that confuses product and action is not effective marketing; a profitable-looking layout based on fake scarcity is unacceptable.

## Understand before choosing sections
Record in a concise brief:
- Audience / segment, current user situation and most important job-to-be-done.
- One primary qualified behavior: start a useful trial, see the product, request a real demo, purchase if functioning, or sign up truthfully.
- Product mechanism, differentiator, alternatives, concrete proof available, objections and non-negotiable claims.
- Traffic intent/source and visitor awareness. Do not presume cold and warm visitors need the same journey.
- CTA destination and what happens after click; signup, onboarding and purchase terms must really work.
- Open unknowns and the owner's source of truth for price, trial, restrictions, claims and testimonials.

## Attention, scene purpose and commercial restraint
The sequence below describes visitor questions, NOT mandatory separate LP sections. Before layout, use the scene ledger in `attention-architecture-and-density.md`: question, authentic new proof, best medium, meaningful next decision, then KEEP/MERGE/CUT/DEFER. Refuse a default stack of benefits, logos, metrics, FAQs and extra CTAs merely to make the page seem complete.
Keep one leading focal point and primary CTA meaning per decision context, allowing honest secondary paths. Balance an immediate action for ready visitors against inspectable evidence for careful visitors. Demonstrate the real product instead of recarding the same claim. Put evidence adjacent to the claims it actually supports. Let personality create recognition, curiosity, confidence and agency, not noise (see `brand-affinity-and-desire.md`). Continue from CTA to genuine first value where in scope (see `activation-retention-and-ethical-engagement.md`). Dense truthful information may be necessary for complex offers; critical price/limits/terms must remain visible and accessible.
## Conversion sequence as hypotheses, not a fixed template
1. First viewport answers: who is this for, what changes, why believe it, what can I do? A strong experience may reveal some answers through a scene; clarity is still required.
2. Demonstrate the **actual product** or credible use case; avoid fake dashboards, unearned AI labels and stock imagery presented as evidence.
3. Earn trust using attributable proof, inspectable features, candid boundaries, product footage and accurately labeled concepts; no fabricated logos or outcomes.
4. Handle likely objections in context: differentiation, setup/time, effort, compatibility, pricing, data/privacy and support, only if material to the audience.
5. Make action coherent with promise and device. A real CTA, correct destination, clear cost/commitment, useful feedback and recovery states.
6. Ending reinforces the user's decision and ties back to the page's story; do not append a generic three-card FAQ/CTA merely to finish a template.

## Copy as art direction
Use specific spoken-language product terms. Ruthlessly remove generic claims like 'unlock your potential', 'revolutionary platform' and repeated advantages with no proof. Translate features into lived user outcomes without inventing statistics. Image, typography, pacing and words form one communication channel. Headlines are judged alongside the product demonstration, not independently.

## Measurement and experiments
Define primary conversion and guardrail metrics before shipping; example: CTA → qualified signup → activated session; separate from raw clicks. Instrument only necessary, consent-respecting events. Check event names, duplicate submissions, attribution and error handling; no invented traffic volume.
- For A/B, hold audience assignment and measurement windows stable, test a coherent causal hypothesis, and avoid peeking at tiny samples as 'winners'. Check guardrails (refunds, accessibility, trust, activation quality).
- Distinguish user research, heuristic CRO, usability observation, and statistically credible live experiment. A template/pattern is not validated for this product until observed.
- Where experiments cannot run, provide plausible hypotheses and indicate UNTESTED. Never claim conversion gains from a screenshot.

## Landing review gates
- Primary message and product recognition; clarity of price, eligibility, terms and CTA.
- Evidence and factual integrity; objection coverage without unnecessary length.
- Opening AND a distinctive truthful middle scene and purposeful ending; repeated claims, competing focal points and unearned ornament need architectural correction, not merely cosmetic restyling.
- Signup/form errors, keyboard focus, screen-reader labels, mobile thumb reach, touch interactions, reduced motion and recovery.
- Distinguish factual FAIL from strategic UNKNOWN. If the product is a speculative concept, do not pretend that buying or subscribing works.
