# Human comprehension and first value — design for people, not for dashboards

This is a short production protocol, not permission to redesign an otherwise good site. Use it when the site is selling to nontechnical humans or when a page looks polished yet does not persuade.

## 1. Human cause, not SaaS features

The first screen must help the visitor answer, without reading instructions:
- **Is this for someone like me?** Show a recognizable situation, not a broad market category.
- **What changes for me?** Make the benefit tangible, not a metric abstraction.
- **What happens if I click?** Match button copy to the real next step and its cost.
- **Can I trust it?** Show a real demonstration or bounded example. Disclose limits, risks, payment timing and registration.
- **Why now?** Only authentic urgency based on a lived deadline, never a fake countdown.

Start copy with the problem the human recognizes: “Tem uma entrevista em inglês chegando?” instead of “Plataforma líder de conversação por IA que usa modelos multimodais”. If the use case is travel, show an airport check-in; don't lead with 14 illustrated cities, a proficiency heatmap or “engagement KPIs.”

## 2. Reverse the AI-slop production order

**Bad process:** assemble headline, dashboard, feature cards, price table, random gradient, icons; then try to make it beautiful.

**Better process:**
1. Observe or retrieve grounded customer language and relevant competitor/customer journeys. Ask what happens in the user's life before the visit.
2. Identify the first *specific* useful outcome the product can truly deliver. Verify in implementation.
3. Find **3–5 excellent contemporary reference pages** by the same decision job (not just same industry). Explain the mechanism: hierarchy, product proof, tone, visual cadence, friction, trust and CTA. Do not assert their conversion data.
4. Compose hero + strongest authentic proof + ending at both desktop and narrow mobile sizes. Distinctive imagery/color/motion derive from the actual product and brand, not trend templates.
5. Build working code and render it. Have the product owner/independent reviewer identify the weakest scene. Change only what improves comprehension, craft or behavior.
6. Run a cold user test and revise. If the requested edit is narrow, use this as a compact check, not a mandatory research expedition.

Sources for patterns to inspect, not copy: `https://www.speak.com/`, `https://praktika.ai/`, `https://www.duolingo.com/`. Check them live for current claims and layouts. NN/g's eyetracking research is useful to ensure the headline and first lines carry meaning: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-scanning-patterns-eyetracking/.

## 3. Five-second and first-use testing

Ask five suitable strangers individually (not other agents posing as people), ideally on their phones, to look at the initial viewport for five seconds. Then hide it. Ask **open questions without coaching**:
- “O que você acha que essa empresa faz?”
- “Para quem isso é?”
- “O que você espera ao apertar o botão?”
- “O que te faria desconfiar ou desistir?”

Show page again and have them attempt one task. Record time from arrival to first value, actual actions, confusing phrases, blockages and unexpected expectations. Five participants is a qualitative diagnostic, **not** a scientifically valid conversion-rate estimate. If no participants available, mark as `HUMAN TEST NOT RUN`; an LLM review is not a substitute.

## 4. Copy and media quality

Use concrete verbs and scenes. Keep technical terms for FAQs, product documentation and interested buyers. Avoid artificial urgency, fake study outcomes, made-up ratings, performative startup superlatives or “as seen on” without proof.

Before-and-after examples (make only claims the product actually supports):

| Internal feature language | Human-facing expression |
|---|---|
| Context-adaptive roleplay | “Treine o que dizer quando o atendente te fizer uma pergunta inesperada.” |
| Speech recognition and LLM feedback | “Fale em inglês. Veja uma dica e tente responder de novo.” |
| Personalized learning path analytics | “Continue de onde parou e pratique aquilo que ainda dá branco.” |
| Gamified retention dashboard | “Volte amanhã e veja se a frase sai mais fácil.” |

The demo must be labeled accurately: a **scripted illustration** is different from a **working live voice demonstration**. A fake interface or an emotional scene unrelated to what is implemented reduces confidence. Visual proof should resemble the product people will actually open.

## 5. Two different quality bars

**Craft bar:** composition, restraint, readable typography, real responsive re-composition, imagery rights, appropriate motion, performance, keyboard/touch/reduced-motion, original visual identity.

**Commercial bar:** comprehension, honest promise, relevance to the ad/search intent, clean CTA path, credible proof, support and terms, successful first-use experience. A page may pass one and fail the other. Reporting must separate subjective critique, observed user tests and measured business conversion.

If there is no qualified acquisition traffic, prioritize a working demonstration, targeted message and channel before polishing 20 sections. A design change does not create an inbound channel by itself.

## 6. Release criterion

Report at minimum: audience and first-use goal; reference mechanism chosen; actual implementation and URLs/screenshots inspected; the single material defect repaired; mobile and functional states verified; whether a real user could grasp the value; and what remains unverified. **Never equate “looks premium” with “sold more.”**
