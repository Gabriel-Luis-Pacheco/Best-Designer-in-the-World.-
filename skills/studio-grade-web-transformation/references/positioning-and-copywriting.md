# Positioning and copywriting — customer truth before clever lines

Activate for marketing sites, product positioning, pricing, service portfolios, case studies, onboarding and any interface where language influences a decision. Do not impose a sales funnel on a technical manual, artwork or internal dashboard.

## 1. Find positioning before writing the headline

Use the following sequence inspired by April Dunford's five components (research basis: https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning):

1. **Competitive alternatives** — what would the buyer actually do without this product? Include doing nothing, manual work or an established habit where supported. Do not list unrelated famous brands merely because they rank for a keyword.
2. **Differentiated capabilities** — what can this product demonstrably do that the real alternatives cannot, or do less well? Keep evidence and uncertainty with each observation.
3. **Customer value** — why would a person care? Translate capability into an observable outcome, not a hypothetical guaranteed improvement.
4. **Best-fit audience** — who urgently values that difference, and under which triggering situation? Avoid fictional demographic personas that pretend to represent research.
5. **Market frame** — choose a familiar category that makes the product easy to understand; invent a new category only with a grounded reason.

Work with at least one real product action, artifact, permissioned demonstration or example. If differentiation is unknown, do not invent it: state a testable hypothesis, demonstrate the authentic core, and avoid exaggerated comparison claims.

## 2. Build the message from actual buyer questions

Write a **message spine**, not an automatic section template:

- **What is it?** Category and useful problem solved, understandable to a cold visitor.
- **Who is it for and when?** A recognizable moment or user job, in the audience's vocabulary.
- **Why this alternative?** Real capability, experience or craft that is materially different.
- **How do I know?** Real demonstration, outcome data with source/method, recognizable portfolio work, product screenshots, testable functionality, authentic client words where authorized.
- **What happens next?** The actual CTA destination, cost/commitment where relevant, expected next step, and recovery from errors.
- **What could hold me back?** Main legitimate objection answered where needed, without an automatic wall of FAQs.

The page may begin with an emotional/abstract headline for an ART or entertainment piece, but the user still needs an appropriately placed concrete identity and accessible path to the real task. Conversion pages must not become puzzles.

## 3. Write copy in seven human passes

1. **Accuracy:** can the source/real product support each meaningful promise? Does pricing match the implementation? No fake testimonials, rankings, scarcity or metrics.
2. **Comprehension:** would a new visitor understand the category, intended audience, difference and action without another paragraph of jargon?
3. **Specificity:** replace empty abstractions with concrete situations, demonstrations and verbs. Not every sentence needs a number or claim.
4. **Audience language:** prefer observed customer vocabulary and job context; avoid internal feature names unless the audience uses them.
5. **Rhythm and voice:** edit for the brand's tone (playful, cinematic, calm, technical, etc.), using deliberate short/long variation rather than the same AI slogan every section.
6. **Skimmability:** lead with relevant information, meaningful headings, short blocks where suitable, enough detail for complicated purchases and legal terms. Do **not** impose a universal character-count or word-count formula.
7. **Microcopy:** CTA states what really happens, form labels and validation prevent errors, loading/success/empty/error states preserve confidence, pricing/limits and cancellation remain visible and understandable.

Research support: NNGroup's writing tests https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-writing-guidelines-web-pages/ and its 2026 microcopy guidance https://www.nngroup.com/articles/3-cs-microcopy/; W3C plain-language objective https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o3-clear-content/.

## 4. Make copy and art direction one system

The real product must be demonstrated, not described repeatedly. Pair each primary claim with the scene, action, film, image or data that shows it. If the same argument appears in two sections, fuse or cut before polishing. A powerful visual line can be short **only if** the accompanying visual actually carries the meaning. Avoid turning every answer into a card or every sentence into a heading. A site can be sparse or richly editorial depending on the user job.

Use headline variants when a concept is unsettled:
- straightforward category + outcome;
- audience or situation + concrete payoff;
- distinctive story-led expression + an immediately legible supporting line.

Do not choose variants based only on an LLM's preference. Test comprehension and recall with representative humans when possible, and preserve brand identity; copy testing and site experiments are different evidence levels. CXL proposes research from analytics, surveys, usability testing and copy testing: https://cxl.com/conversion-optimization/conversion-research/.

## 5. Guardrails before shipping

- Claims trace to source evidence and legal/commercial contracts. Supplied-but-unverified information remains unverified.
- Every material outcome or comparison differentiator must have a corresponding inspectable proof or be explicitly labeled a hypothesis.
- CTA and offer must match actual route, behavior, payment, trial, availability and follow-up.
- Don't conflate lead count with qualified leads or click rate with conversion, trust, retention or sales.
- Optimize for truthful qualified intent. Avoid manipulative friction, dark patterns, keyword stuffing and overclaiming.
- For low-stakes portfolio/art sites, distinctive personality can lead; conversion mechanics should be unobtrusive.

Use `scripts/positioning_copy_contract.py` with a position/proof/message JSON to reject unsupported declarations. Passing means ready for human copy testing, not that the writing is persuasive or sales have improved.
