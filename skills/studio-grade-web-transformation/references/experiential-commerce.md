# Experiential Commerce — commerce with artistic ambition

Use when a real product landing must *both* trigger wonder and make the product desirable or understandable. This is a first-class composition problem, not a conventional ecommerce template with decorative parallax. It must not override explicit user objectives or applicable contracts.

## The two promises
**Artistic:** create a memorable sensory idea that could not belong to an arbitrary product. **Commercial:** make it clear what the object is, why someone might want it, what is real or conceptual, and what the next action actually does.

Neither promise automatically trumps the other. When the user explicitly prioritizes art, art leads. When they explicitly prioritize conversion, conversion leads. Do not mistake a speculative gallery for a functioning checkout.

## Scene before sales funnel
1. **Object truth:** physical/digital shape, differentiator, what is fictional, real price/availability if known; no fabricated quotes or claims.
2. **Visual thesis:** derive behavior from the product's mechanism. For a sculptural speaker, sound can materialize as light, shape or vibration. A smartphone ad cannot use the exact same staging unchanged.
3. **Decide the whole arc:** opening → desirable object reveal → concept made tangible → useful details in an expressive language → memorable climax → credible next action.
4. **Commercial reveal:** make it possible to understand the actual product, not only perceive a mysterious shape. Include legible, accurately described benefits or experience; do not bury every fact in animation.
5. **Interaction:** rotate, perturb, compose or reveal what makes the product special. Some moments should change the world, not control an isolated widget.
6. **CTA integrity:** a CTA without backend cannot claim successful subscription or payment. Show a truthful conceptual next step instead.
7. **Mobile direction:** choreograph separately for touch, constrained aspect ratios, slow devices and reduced motion. Avoid thumbnailing desktop art.
8. **Full-page review:** compare first impression, visual variety, product legibility, object desire, interaction quality, climax, ending, action clarity and performance. If a middle section looks generic, challenge its scene—not just spacing.

## Avoid both bad extremes
- **Spectacle with no product:** beautiful environment but no identifiable object, benefit, or intended decision.
- **Ordinary landing in costume:** hero renders spectacularly but below-fold content becomes repetitive card grids and template CTAs.

A good outcome preserves *both* an authored visual grammar and a truthful commercial story.

## Execution economy
Inspect product facts and constraints, make two meaningfully different creative directions, choose one, build a representative opening/middle/end at desktop+mobile, then implement and review the full page. Do not spend the majority of a finite agent budget filling templates or reviewing references without constructing the experience. A real browser observation is evidence; an elaborate written critique is not.

## Evidence and limitations
A successful build, screenshot diff or a passing Playwright smoke test does not prove creative excellence or sales conversion. Compare with a baseline under matched conditions, review blind, and treat user taste reports as qualitative feedback until supported by artifacts. Do not invent A/B results, frame rates or product efficacy.
