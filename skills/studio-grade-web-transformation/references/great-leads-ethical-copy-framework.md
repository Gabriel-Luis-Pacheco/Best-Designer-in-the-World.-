# Great Leads — ethical landing and sales-message opening playbook

Inspired by the **conceptual framework** of *Great Leads: The Six Easiest Ways to Start Any Sales Message*, by Michael Masterson and John Forde. This is a concise **original adaptation**, not a reproduction, excerpt, or substitute for the book. Source background: https://www.awai.com/2012/01/just-released-great-leads/ ; historical summary/reference: https://www.theoldseller.com/great-leads . Use only for relevant commercial/educational copy (hero, product introduction, paid ad, email, sales page, video opening), never by default on documentation or operational interfaces.

## The message problem

A visitor is not an abstract "lead"; they arrive with different levels of familiarity, doubts and commitment. First determine how aware they are of:
1. Their own problem or unmet outcome.
2. Available solutions and substitutes.
3. This product and its real distinguishing capability.
4. The actual offer, price, decision and next action.

Then select **one meaningful organizing idea** grounded in the product, not a bundle of slogans. Decide whether a **direct** introduction is helpful (high product/offer awareness) or an **indirect** opening is useful (less awareness, provided the visitor can still quickly understand what is being offered). The more difficult a promise is to verify, the more evidence should be shown before claiming it.

## Six families of opening — choose one as a hypothesis, not a template

| Family | Useful when | Honest version | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Offer** | Audience is already product/offer aware | Show real product, concrete offer, actual terms and CTA | Artificial discount, hidden conditions |
| **Promise** | Specific desired result with credible support | Lead with one evidence-backed benefit | Fabricated timelines, guarantees or "be fluent in 7 days" |
| **Problem → solution** | A recognized frustration strongly affects purchase | Name a real problem and demonstrate actual mechanism | Agitating fears or diagnosing user problems without evidence |
| **Big secret / discovery** | There is a real, non-obvious insight demonstrable for free | Show useful discovery and explain the product's role | Fake "secret", information withheld only to force a click |
| **Proclamation** | A defensible surprising fact changes the conversation | Make a precise, supportable observation | Hyperbole, fake studies, unsupported market leadership |
| **Story** | Authentic journey explains the customer job | Truthful product or user story with clear relevance | Made-up testimonials or stories that conceal the offer |

Do not combine all six headings on one landing page. Sometimes a plain, direct value statement will perform better than any elaborate framework.

## Conversion from strategy to actual UI

1. Identify page origin: exact campaign, keyword intent, referral, social context or known user step (UNKNOWN if not available).
2. Record awareness hypothesis and why; don't state that you "know" the visitor's thoughts.
3. Write **one sentence** on the product mechanism and why the buyer cares; list real proof assets.
4. Prototype **two materially different** opening hypotheses, e.g. direct demo/offer vs relevant problem/solution. Keep the rest of the experience consistent when comparison needs to isolate the variable.
5. On the hero and first scroll: show subject, evidence and legible next action. Design supports the promise rather than replacing it with abstract shapes, lengthy copy or 3D effects.
6. Validate usability, trust and factual truth. Run observed comprehension checks or appropriately designed tests when possible. Otherwise classify preference and conversion as UNKNOWN.

### Original hypothetical example (not a real BataTalk feature claim)
If a fictional language product offers documented *guided voice practice* and nothing else is verified:
- **Offer-style:** "Pratique inglês em conversas guiadas." Follow immediately with a real demonstration and accurate plan terms.
- **Problem-solution:** "Você entende inglês, mas trava na hora de falar?" Follow with a real guided practice clip and accurate recovery step.
- **Story-style:** Show an authentic, authorized story of trying again with useful feedback, if genuine proof is available. Never invent a success testimonial.

These are optional hypotheses, not validated headlines or performance predictions.

## Evaluation

Score no headline solely on emotional impact. Check: message-to-audience fit, specificity, truth/proof, immediate comprehension, accessibility and relationship to first useful value. Measure downstream activation and qualified conversion rather than shallow clickbait. Keep legal/health/financial/educational outcome claims within actual proof; avoid manipulative urgency, vulnerable-user pressure, and false scarcity.

The Great Leads framework is about deciding **how to begin a relevant communication**, not replacing research, product value, source reliability, accessible UX or ethical design. Use Marketing Skills Copywriting/CRO for specialized execution only when those skills are truly available.
