# Attention architecture — exceptional landings without visual clutter

Load for LANDING, acquisition, campaigns, pricing and commercial home pages. This is a decision method, not a mandatory page template. Clutter is unnecessary competition for attention, not a fixed word count. Minimalism that hides price, product limitations or proof is also a defect.

## Five-part signal contract
Before layout, identify: **VISITOR** (intent, awareness, context); **PROMISE** (truthful outcome); **PROOF** (real product behavior or verifiable evidence); **ACTION** (one useful primary next step at each decision point and its real destination); **MEMORY** (product-specific form, line, scene or gesture that makes the brand recognizable). Mark absent facts UNKNOWN, not invented.

## Scene ledger, then subtract
For each proposed section or scene, write only: visitor question → genuinely new answer/proof/emotion → best medium → next decision → KEEP/MERGE/CUT/DEFER. The ledger is an internal aid, never a substitute for implementation.
- Merge scenes that restate one advantage using different generic illustrations or cards.
- Cut unsupported social proof, ornamental stats, decorative pricing teasers, fake demos and SEO-like paragraphs nobody needs for this decision.
- Defer detailed explanation only when the user can still find it, and never conceal critical terms, restrictions, risks or accessibility information.
- Avoid default stacks of hero + logos + 3 benefit cards + stats + quotes + FAQ + duplicated CTA. Any of these can be useful **when earned by authentic evidence or a user question**.
- Use rich proof early enough to justify the promise. For warm traffic, preserve an efficient path to action; for careful visitors, provide an honest evidence path.
- A long-form page can be right for complex B2B, regulated or comparison decisions. A short page can be right for a simple verified product.

## Viewport-level attention budget
At hero, each major transition, pricing and mobile, ask: **what must the eye see first, second and third?** Prefer one dominant focal point and one primary decision meaning at a time; smaller navigation or evidence can remain visible. Equal-salience CTAs, gradients, floating badges, avatars, illustrations and giant words compete. If everything shouts, create an actual hierarchy by adjusting contrast, scale, grouping, silence and motion, not by deleting useful facts.

A high-salience element must pay for itself by improving understanding, justified desire, trust or action. Whitespace improves grouping only when supported by content; vast emptiness and oversized headings that hide a thin offer are not craft. Cards work for comparisons or independent items, not as an obligatory layout unit. Motion should reveal causality, feedback or authored transformation, never hide meaning or delay action.

## Four rapid critiques
1. **Blur/squint:** after suppressing fine text, which element owns each viewport? Does the eye move in the intended order?
2. **Five-second recognition:** could someone identify the product, its role and next step? This is a heuristic unless tested with people.
3. **Fifteen-second credibility:** does a visitor encounter authentic product behavior, inspectable proof or transparent limitation? Again, not a proven population result.
4. **Full-page silhouette:** opening, meaningful middle and decision/ending. Identify repetitive section outlines and same-weight imagery. Inspect intentional mobile recomposition, not merely vertical stacking.

## Replace bad patterns with a coherent mechanism
**Fictional typed language tutor:** not giant slogan → three benefits → three more benefits → fake testimonials. Instead, specific promise → one real typed prompt/answer/feedback/retry demonstration → necessary limits/prices and honest next step. If screenshots or functionality are unavailable, label a concept as conceptual.
**B2B technical service:** not a mysterious glossy scene plus ten panels. Instead, reveal actual workflow/output, verification, constraints and appropriate demo entry. Useful detail can be dense.
**Expressive launch:** keep an authored visual idea across beginning, middle and ending without burying product recognition or truthful action. Intentional artistic exploration can justify a longer scene sequence.

## Release and evidence
Perform a **CUT/MERGE pass before polishing** and identify which section disappeared or became a real visual/product interaction. Test the actual CTA, pricing truth, keyboard and focus, 320/390px mobile, reduced motion, errors and measured speed when possible. A static analyzer may suggest repeated cards or excessive text, but cannot judge composition or aesthetics. Mark visual, conversion and retention outcomes UNKNOWN without real evidence.
