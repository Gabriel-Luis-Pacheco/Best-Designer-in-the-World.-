# Creative Advantage Loop — freedom with a higher quality ceiling

Use for high-ambition ART/EXPERIENCE, brand campaigns, experiential commerce, and systemic REDESIGN/REBUILD. For PATCH, backoffice and small maintenance, do NOT impose this loop. This is creative direction, not an automated taste algorithm.

## The central mistake to avoid
A skill can raise the quality floor yet lower the ceiling: it may make an agent overly cautious, formulaic and bureaucratic. Therefore protect truth, accessibility and working flows as **hard constraints**, but never treat default layouts, a preferred palette, conservative spatial composition or a fixed number of sections as constraints. The strongest justified visual direction wins, not the safest interpretation of the checklist.

## Diverge quickly, then build
1. Identify **product verb**, recognizable object, target reaction, visitor's intended action, brand invariants, and genuine proof. For art-only work define the emotional ending instead of a commercial CTA.
2. Generate **up to three short creative hypotheses** for high-ambition work; render **two genuinely distinct visual proofs** if tools/budget permit. For ordinary structural redesigns, two hypotheses and one chosen direction are usually enough. Do not add third concept just for procedure. A hypothesis must differ across at least TWO of: space/geometry, editorial voice, narrative order, signature gesture, product demonstration, material system, or mobile choreography. Merely swapping color or fonts fails. Keep this short; never build three complete websites.
3. Visualize rough whole-journey silhouettes if rendering tools exist. Each must cover OPENING / RECOGNITION / MIDDLE / INTERACTION / CLIMAX / CLOSURE / MOBILE, adapted to the actual task. A paragraph describing "premium/minimal" is not an art direction. A real first-frame thumbnail is more useful than a ten-page manifesto.
4. Compare candidate directions using **observed or explicitly hypothetical** strengths: first-frame magnetism, unmistakable identity, product recognition, demonstration/trust, editorial composition, interaction payoff, ending, mobile viability, feasibility. Reject hard failures first; among viable options, select the most distinctive and causally appropriate, not the most templated.
5. Lock a small visual grammar (type voice, material, image treatment, layout physics, motion cause, color role) and a large possibility space for section architecture. **Consistency does not mean identical composition.** Build the strongest opening, a non-generic middle, the defining interaction and a meaningful closing on desktop and mobile as soon as possible.

## Compete against your own best alternative
Once real pixels exist, perform an **adversarial studio pass**:
- Pretend a rival agent is free of every template. What single more memorable scene, better type decision, bolder composition or stronger reveal could it make?
- If the concept can be transplanted unchanged to an unrelated company, redesign its visual mechanism, not just the copy.
- Identify ONE scene whose presence drags down the whole sequence; rebuild it at its causal level. Do not self-award a 3/3.
- If the opener is spectacular but the middle turns into generic cards, the concept is incomplete. If the page is striking but nobody can identify the actual product, add grounded recognition without flattening the art.
- Compare the revised render at matched viewport and state. An imagined challenger is a thought experiment, NOT a real with/without-skill result.

## Editorial and spatial ambition
Make typography a compositional material: contrast expressive/display and utilitarian voices when appropriate, design deliberate line breaks, optical alignments, variable density, punctuated silences and asymmetry. No obligatory grotesk, giant headings, gradients, dark mode or bento cards. Unexpected crops, editorial layering, full-bleed worlds, constrained columns and type-driven scenes are options, not commands. Every design decision must support its particular content.

Scenes should advance a story instead of resetting to another component grid. Use transformations with physical/narrative continuity: camera, object, lighting, material, gesture or typographic motif can carry across. A static state must still communicate when animation is disabled.

## Product recognition and payoff
An exceptional concept has to be *identifiable*, not only mysterious. At opening/product-reveal, ask a fresh reviewer: "What is this, what distinguishes it, and what can I do?" Record a real observation if available, or mark UNKNOWN. An abstract 3D hero may need a revealing angle, parts, grounded label, context or scale reference.
The last act should pay off what visitors explored. When commerce/back-end is absent, offer a truthful, relevant artifact (e.g., chosen configuration as SVG/render/shareable code or a coherent specification) **only if implemented**. Never invent purchase, waitlist success or delivery. Avoid defaulting to a raw TXT download when a meaningful artifact is genuinely possible. A plain text document is appropriate where text is the product.

## Review 6 actual frames
Opening; recognition; genuinely useful middle; interaction before/after; climax/closing; 390px opening AND one middle/interactive scene. Add 320px if layout is tight, keyboard and reduced-motion states, and verify no text/object collision. For 3D see the dedicated immersive pack. A long full-page screenshot may capture the wrong temporal state in pinned/animated scenes; use representative fixed frames and matching video interaction scripts.

## Budget and stop rule
Timebox divergence; spend the major effort building and revising. Do not make every experiment a three-direction pitch deck. Two genuine render/critique iterations are preferred for large creative tasks, not a universal requirement for isolated fixes. Stop after critical functional and factual gates pass and there is no actionable major creative weakness left **within the authorized scope**, or report the unresolved weakness honestly.

## Defensible evaluation
Visual taste is judged by humans with evidence, not a script. A/B superiority needs independent comparable executions and blind reviews. One win or loss cannot guarantee future wins. Use the optional journey board for matched screenshots; it identifies evidence gaps, never decides aesthetic quality.
