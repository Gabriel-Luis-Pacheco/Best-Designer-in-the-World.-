# Creative Production Loop — faça melhor, não apenas documente melhor

**Contrato de produção.** Em REDESIGN/REBUILD, a deliverable is a functioning page or prototype, not a strategy PDF. Never exhaust the execution budget making templates. During substantial implementation, reserve approximate attention for: 10% product/constraints, 15% benchmark + two theses, 45% build, 25% inspect/rebuild, 5% clear handoff. These are **planning heuristics, not timers or quotas**; move effort to implementation whenever the project is clear.

## 1 — Two materially different creative hypotheses
A hypothesis is NOT an alternate palette. For each, sketch a 5-second opener, **middle proof**, ending/decision, mobile adaptation and signature interaction. Change at least two of: the content model, spatial grammar, method of proof, narrative rhythm, animation metaphor. State why the idea is specific to this product. Choose quickly; never force three ideas for a patch.

## 2 — Build a vertical slice
Implement a *representative* hero, middle section and closing/action in one coherent CSS/image system; add a mobile composition. In a new artistic project the slice must include the **climax**, not only the opener. Use real product facts/assets; concept assets must be labeled. A site that only looks good in its first 900px fails the slice.

## 3 — Inspect what actually rendered
Capture opening, middle, weakest transition, final section and mobile. Compare screenshots from matching browser/viewport/font/scroll state. Inspect text hierarchy, asset styling, negative space, compositional tension, personality, working interactions, perceived continuity and copy density. In a creative-only experience inspect meaningful gesture, transformation and climax. In commercial projects inspect product understanding, demonstration, action and commercial truth.

## 4 — Correct by causal level
- **Architecture:** wrong narrative, excessive or redundant sections => remove/fuse/rebuild scenes.
- **Art direction:** hero and rest feel like separate brands => unify visual grammar and remake weaker scenes.
- **Asset:** off-brand mascot, generic characters, fake product imagery => fix using canon; do not mask in gradient.
- **Component:** poor states, generic cards, focus, layout => replace or refine component.
- **Technical:** unreadable mobile, broken flow, performance => repair and re-test.
Never solve a macro architecture failure with a dozen shadows or font-size tweaks.

## 5 — A second rendered iteration is the minimum meaningful loop
After inspection, make a *material* correction to the weakest scene. Capture again and record before/after with the cause of improvement or remaining concern. If the original was already strong, justify preserving it, with adjacent regression tests. **Do not claim a review happened from an unrendered plan.** No hard promise of awards or conversion.

## Stop conditions
Stop when product truth, interactions, accessibility, mobile and coherent art direction meet the brief and no actionable P0/P1 is left; or when constrained by time/tools, report outstanding blockers. For product work protect routes and contracts; for pure art, preserve artistic intent and user control. Both require honesty about tests.

## Senior critique prompts
- What *only this product* could have made this scene?
- What one visual event will someone remember?
- If copy is hidden and logo removed, does the visual signature remain?
- What is the weakest 20% of the scroll and why?
- Is there a meaningful progression from beginning → middle → ending?
- Is the mobile composition intentionally redrawn?
- Does interaction transform understanding, not merely toggle a card?
- Did the second iteration improve a real observed weakness?
