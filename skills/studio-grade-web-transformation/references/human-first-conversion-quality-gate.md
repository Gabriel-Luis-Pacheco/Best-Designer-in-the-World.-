# Human-first conversion and taste gate

> Activate for any landing page, paywall, onboarding, product positioning, brand/motion redesign or commercial website. This is a practical evaluation contract, not an invitation to fill the page with generic CRO widgets. The real person makes the decision, not the designer or code.

## 0. Understand the business before drawing

Observe the **actual current artifact** and customer journey. Write one sentence each: (1) who is arriving and from where, (2) what they are trying to do, (3) what problem they already know they have, (4) what they currently do instead, (5) what first useful action is possible today, and (6) what promise we can truly prove. Distinguish verified from invented. If none of these answers is evidenced, create a provisional hypothesis and test it before rebuilding everything.

For BataTalk, the working hypothesis is an adult who can understand some English but freezes when speaking. This is a hypothesis until validated by real users; the product is voice practice in everyday scenes with help, not an enterprise AI dashboard. Do not call it fluency, human tutoring, limitless conversation or guaranteed results unless substantiated.

## 1. Human decision flow

From arrival to first value, independently inspect:

1. **Recognition:** Does the first screen use the person's vocabulary? "I understand but freeze when I speak" beats "AI-driven language dashboard" *as an untested hypothesis*, not as a universally proven winning headline.
2. **Comprehension:** Can a newcomer correctly answer "What is it?", "Is it for me?", "What can I do now?" after five seconds?
3. **Motivation:** Can they connect the claimed benefit to a concrete moment (ordering a coffee, job interview), rather than admire a generic list of capabilities?
4. **Credibility:** Is there a real demo, factual product screenshot, understandable limits, actual price and understandable privacy? Never invent reviews, ratings, logos, growth figures, people or outcomes.
5. **Friction:** Does the primary button lead to working first value with minimal avoidable steps? Try keyboard, low-end mobile, in-app browsers, mic denied, audio unavailable, slow network, registration, price/cancel.
6. **Return value:** Does the paid plan preserve or improve the most loved parts of the free experience? Treat aggressive upsell and low-quality AI responses as potential churn drivers.

Read **real human words** from interviews or support messages; if missing, mark unknown rather than generating faux "customer quotes". Use distinct tasks and put actual users through it. A simulated persona is a *preflight*, not validation.

## 2. Study outstanding human-made work correctly

For a significant redesign inspect **3–6 distinctive reference experiences**, including at least one direct competitor and one excellent unrelated product; more only when uncertainty remains. Reference protocol:

| Question | Evidence |
|---|---|
| What exact visitor uncertainty does a section resolve? | Screenshot, URL, date and visible interaction |
| Why might a human understand and trust it? | Hierarchy, layout, word choice, real evidence, timing |
| What is merely fashion? | Gradient, glass blur, generic bento, fake dashboard, meaningless animation |
| What belongs to the original brand? | Type, visual language, pacing, space, emotional context |
| What would NOT transfer to BataTalk? | Business-model mismatch, false proof, higher ticket, enterprise CTA |
| What could improve our own first action? | Testable hypothesis, expected behavior and measurement |

Don't reproduce their protected imagery, animations or layout verbatim. Building *like* a prestigious company means matching the craft, coherence and constraints, not pasting a corporate template.

When revisiting an already strong page, preserve its identity and approved elements; don't make an entire new LP because somebody dislikes a single panel. Compare the actual current screenshot with concrete references before issuing a diff.

## 3. Copy rules for people, not a compiler

- Lead with **someone's problem, desired progress and a demonstrable action**; use nouns they recognize.
- Put technical labels (API, LLM, AI pipeline, features, dashboard) only where a real decision requires them.
- One clear promise per visible scene. If two scenes prove the same thing, combine; if neither adds evidence, remove.
- A specific live demo can outperform many illustrations — verify that it works; do not disguise a mocked animation as real usage.
- Strong verbs and specific language, not superlatives. A company isn't trustworthy because it describes itself as "revolutionary", "premium", "state-of-the-art".
- Explain limits plainly, especially paid quotas and handling of users' voices. Avoid surprise costs, fake urgency and dark patterns.

**Two examples for designers to reject:**

BAD hero: "Unlock AI-powered speaking performance with intelligent dashboards, real-time analytics and gamified UX."
BETTER hypothesis: "Você entende inglês, mas trava quando precisa falar? Ensaie uma conversa em voz alta, no seu ritmo." Working CTA: "Falar minha primeira frase." The better one is not a claim of measured conversion.

BAD trust block: three fabricated 5-star reviews, "100k users", stock portrait.
BETTER: a genuine interactive scene, transparent explanation of audio/feedback, verifiable privacy and real prices; authentic testimonials only after consent.

## 4. Artistic quality is part of usability

Reject generic production: standard three rounded cards, emoji icons, stock photos, huge wasted whitespace, uniform sections, fake product UI, indiscriminate gradients, overpolished synthetic characters, purposeless motion and "enterprise dashboards" for consumers.

Produce a defensible visual direction **derived from the product's subject**: the conversational back-and-forth, feeling of hesitant speaking, comforting guidance, lived-in environments, distinct mascot identity. Art direction must include composition, typography, material, contrast, pacing, mobile layout, transitions and touch behavior. Judge first fold and long-scroll rhythm in actual pixels.

Measure separately:
- **Craft:** legibility, visual hierarchy, balance, restraint, distinctive art, clean detail, consistent character forms.
- **Comprehension:** correct explanation of product and next step by an unaffiliated person.
- **Conversion journey:** actual CTA to first spoken response and meaningful feedback.
- **Trust:** real evidence, honest conditions, no AI-looking fake proof.
- **Performance/accessibility:** loading, mobile, focus, reduced motion, mic fallback.

Do not average these into one self-declared "10/10" score. Technical green checks do not certify beauty. Beautiful images do not certify conversion.

## 5. Mandatory evaluation artifacts for major LP redesigns

1. Baseline: screenshots desktop/mobile + date/branch/version + the current promise, the main CTA and the actual step reached.
2. Reference ledger: 3–6 references and **specific transferable insights**, not a dump of design links.
3. Explicit user comprehension test: recruit 3–5 eligible humans if available (qualitative only); ask unaided "what is this and what happens if you click?" Collect literal answers with consent. No access means `NOT_TESTED`.
4. The diff and new screenshots, **including largest weak section and mobile**, after correction.
5. Tested path from landing CTA through first value and a transparent paid path when relevant.
6. Hypothesis to measure: CTA->first spoken response, drop-off, error rate, return and purchases by period. Only use valid denominators; don't claim uplift without comparing real outcomes.
7. Independent review: designers, human observer or reviewer with no incentive to approve the work; report failed criteria and revision.
8. Final decision: KEEP, REVISE, ROLLBACK or WAIT_FOR_HUMAN_DATA. A good product may need new distribution instead of a redesign.

### When not to redesign

If the current hero already names the pain in human language, has a functioning demo and clear CTA, and there is little traffic or no observed confusion, **acquisition and user research may have higher expected value**. Make that call. The job is business performance and product experience, not filling an AI agent's schedule.

### Unit economics affects creative

Make product promise and price consistent with real quotas and quality. No hidden restrictions; don't degrade core user experience to shave AI cost without evaluating cancellation/retention. A customer who leaves because the product disappointed them is not made profitable by saving a few cents.

## 6. Evidence anchors (methodological, not conversion guarantees)

- Nielsen Norman Group (usability and writing): https://www.nngroup.com/topic/writing-web/ — scanability and direct user understanding.
- Y Combinator: https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-startup-advice — work with customers and iterate actual usage.
- Google Search Central: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content — useful, reliable, original human-focused content.
- Google AI Search: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide — fundamentals, originality and clear information rather than GEO shortcuts.

No named design pattern or book automatically proves conversion. Only observed human response and valid business data can substantiate the outcome.
