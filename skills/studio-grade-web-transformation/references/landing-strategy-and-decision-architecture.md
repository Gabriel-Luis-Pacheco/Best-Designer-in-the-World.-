# Landing strategy and decision architecture — decide before decorating

**Load when:** acquisition landing, signup/waitlist, pricing, service inquiry or experiential commerce. For an isolated PATCH, only use the relevant subsection. This is a set of decisions, **not** a mandatory deliverable, fixed scroll template or instruction to write lengthy documentation. Preserve actual brand, routes, pricing and product contracts.

## 1. Build a small evidence-backed decision model

Before committing to hero art, identify in the existing project or provided brief:

| Decision | What to determine | Unknown handling |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | A concrete best-fit buyer/user and situation that triggers the need | Mark hypothesis, never invent interviews |
| Visitor state | Branded, problem-aware, solution-aware, comparison, skeptical, ready-to-act | Infer cautiously from traffic source; offer paths for different states |
| Status quo | What they do *instead* (including nothing/manual workaround) | Do not name competitors without evidence |
| Offer | What actually happens, what it costs, eligibility, limits, deadlines and obligations | Source of truth is product + approved commercial terms |
| Mechanism | A working thing that produces the offered benefit | Show real UI, artifact, workflow, result, sample or verified case |
| Differentiator | Credible reason to choose this instead of a real alternative | No fake unique/fastest/best/No. 1 claims |
| Proof | Where each consequential claim is demonstrated or verified | `VERIFIED`, `SUPPLIED_UNVERIFIED`, `INFERRED`, `CONCEPT`, `UNKNOWN` |
| Primary action | One commercially meaningful next step, its destination, commitment and failure path | Test the full route, not only click |
| Success | Qualified action downstream, plus guardrails | Unmeasured outcomes remain `UNKNOWN` |

**Evidence hierarchy:** real runtime/records and authorized artifacts > source-traceable third-party information > supplied claims not yet verified > model inference > imagined example. Public social proof only with correct authorization and attribution. A working product sample can be stronger than a decorative logo strip.

## 2. Preserve message match across the entire promise

Map: **arrival source → expected question → truthful first-frame response → proof scene → CTA wording → destination → first value**. The same claim and commitment must survive each hop.

- Search "praticar conversação em inglês" needs a visible speaking/conversation practice mechanism, not only a generic "learn English faster" slogan.
- Campaign "compare fees" needs fees/conditions and a comparison, not an unexplained cinematic intro.
- Branded traffic may tolerate a distinctive expressive opening; a cold visitor still needs identification and a discoverable next action.
- A ready visitor needs a direct, truthful path; a skeptical visitor needs optional evidence without being forced through a cinematic sequence.
- For high-risk/high-cost offers, make price, terms, privacy, cancellation, limitations and uncertainty accessible at the decision point. Never hide important details for "minimalism."

Ask the useful question of *each* distinct scene: **Which belief or uncertainty changes after it, through which new piece of evidence?** If the response repeats a previous scene, MERGE or CUT. A UI component (FAQ, card, testimonial, stat, sticky CTA, comparison table) is optional; use it only for a specific information job. Artistic suspense is legitimate when the user's primary job is art/entertainment, not a default conversion tax.

## 3. Choose the right architecture for the actual visitor

| Surface / buyer | Main uncertainty | Strongest possible proof medium | Sensible next step | Likely failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Self-serve SaaS | "Will it do my job quickly?" | Real interactive workflow, truthful interface and first output | Try real core action / start qualified trial | Hero renders fake UI; onboarding never reaches value |
| B2B enterprise | "Will this integrate, stay secure, pay off?" | Capability workflow, docs, comparable case, technical limits | Focused demo, technical review or contact | Lead form asks too much and claims lack sources |
| B2C education | "Can *I* actually practise/learn?" | Genuine interactive practice slice, feedback/retry, accessible transcript | Try example / sign up | Animated mascot dominates, core practice remains invisible |
| E-commerce | "Is this the right item at the true total cost?" | Actual product angles, specs, options, reviews and delivery/return terms | Choose variant and purchase | Fake scarcity, hidden costs, broken variant state |
| Premium service / portfolio | "Can this person deliver my kind of work?" | Authenticated case, actual footage/work, process and scope | See case / request scoped quote | Effects substitute for work; contact button doesn't work |
| Waitlist / prelaunch | "Why share my email before a product exists?" | Honest concept/demo and development status | Join clearly defined waitlist | Placeholder presented as already operating |
| Art / interactive experience | "Why is this worth exploring?" | Specific authored world and meaningful interactive payoff | Enter / explore / skip appropriately | Pretentious delay and no accessible fallback |

These are **conditional patterns**, not performance guarantees. Use the user's constraints and the evidence to choose.

## 4. Design the journey as a sequence of belief changes

Construct a *provisional*, brief internal storyboard only if direction is unsettled:

1. **Recognition:** can the visitor identify category and relevance from actual first-frame composition? A "five-second" review is a heuristic until tested with representative visitors.
2. **Desire or task fit:** what real situation, unmet need or cultural feeling makes the product compelling?
3. **Mechanism:** show the product, work or behavior that makes the promise plausible. Prefer one compelling proof over many generic feature cards.
4. **Distinction:** demonstrate how this experience differs from the status quo, with defensible facts and design character.
5. **Risk reduction:** resolve the serious objection where it occurs (price, time, suitability, access, data, rights).
6. **Action and continuation:** literal CTA -> real destination -> accessible loading/error/success -> first useful value when in scope.
7. **Memory/ending:** coherent payoff, not an extra CTA section added because a template expects one.

The order may be rearranged, combined, omitted or branched: a checkout needs immediate pricing; art may need a reveal; an enterprise buyer may need documentation before contact.

## 5. Work backwards from failure and correct at the right level

- High polish + wrong visitor/subject? **Return to the original brief and concept**; do not fix shadows.
- Very clear but forgettable? **Find a product-specific physical, linguistic, content or interaction signature**; do not paste a trendy visual style.
- Very expressive but misleading? **Rebuild the visual proof/offer alignment**, not only enlarge CTA.
- Many identical cards/sections? **CUT / MERGE / CONVERT into product demonstration** before increasing complexity.
- CTA clicks but no activation? **Inspect post-click flow and instrumentation** rather than promising a better button color.
- No customer evidence? **Prototype and label hypotheses**; do not manufacture winning conversion claims.

## 6. Minimum output contract for commercial redesigns

A successful delivery is an implemented or clearly declared conceptual experience with a recognizable product/service, genuine proof, working and understandable next action, purposeful full-page progression, intentional mobile layout, accurate terms, accessible states and evidence of what was actually checked. Do not turn the checklist into customer-facing page content. Record blockers and `UNKNOWN` honestly.
